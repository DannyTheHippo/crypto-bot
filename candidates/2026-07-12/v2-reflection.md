## regime notes
Favor acting when price is trending with confluence across timeframes: the base-interval EMA fast is above EMA slow and RSI14 is above 50, ideally agreeing with the 1h/4h EMA trend when available. Treat choppy, range-bound conditions (EMA fast and slow crossing repeatedly, RSI oscillating near 50) as low-edge and prefer holding. In the small recent sample, the two long entries both produced negative next-bar returns (mean about -21bps), and all three closed round trips lost money net of fees — a tentative signal that recent "trend" signals were mostly shallow chop dressed up as trend. Until entries show a positive edge again, raise the bar for what counts as a tradable trend and lean toward flat/hold in anything that is not clearly and persistently directional.

## entry rules
Only enter long when trend and momentum agree AND the move is decisively directional, not a borderline cross: EMA fast clearly above EMA slow (not hovering at the crossover), RSI14 above 50 but below 70, and ideally the higher-timeframe (1h/4h) EMA trend agreeing. Critically, the expected move must clear a comfortable multiple of the ~20bps round-trip fee — recent round trips opened on moves that barely covered costs and all closed net-negative, so require the projected favorable move to be several times the fee cost before entering. Wait for a fresh confirming close rather than reacting to a single wick. When in doubt about whether trend is real, stay flat: recent stayed-flat and held decisions modestly outperformed fresh entries.

## exit rules
Exit to flat when the trend that justified the entry breaks: EMA fast crosses back below EMA slow, RSI14 drops below 45, or unrealized PnL gives back a meaningful share of its peak gain. Note that realized exits filled slightly worse than the decide-time close (a couple of bps of adverse slippage per trip); do not count on capturing the last bp of an exit, and act on a clear break rather than waiting for a borderline one. When unsure whether the break is real or noise, prefer flat over holding a position whose thesis no longer holds.

## mistakes to avoid
Do not overtrade small, noisy fluctuations — each round trip costs real fees, and every recent closed trip lost money net of fees. Do not enter on borderline moves that only barely exceed round-trip costs; small realized edges get erased by fees and adverse slippage. Do not chase price after a move already past typical overbought RSI levels. Do not act on a thin, low-confidence edge — the recent low-confidence longs lost on average. Do not treat these few losses as proof of a permanent regime change; they are thin, noisy evidence, so tighten entry quality rather than abandoning the trend framework wholesale.
